Lowest realised volatility (researcher's own words, 2026-10-05)

Hypothesis: stocks with the lowest trailing realised volatility may generate superior
risk-adjusted forward returns (the low-risk anomaly: Ang, Hodrick, Xing and Zhang 2006;
Baker, Bradley and Wurgler 2011). NSE publishes indices built on the same idea (the
NIFTY Low Volatility 30 family selects the 30 lowest-volatility stocks by one-year daily
standard deviation). The index is research context only, not equivalent evidence: its
universe, weighting, rebalancing, execution and costs differ.

Rules as decided by the researcher:
- Universe: today's NSE F&O list (current membership, not point-in-time; survivorship bias).
- On the last session of each calendar month, after the close, compute each stock's sample
  standard deviation (ddof 1) of its last 252 daily close-to-close returns
  r_t = Close_t / Close_(t-1) - 1 on adjusted closes. Not annualised.
- 252 returns need 253 consecutive closes on the official NSE sessions. A stock missing any
  session in that window is not ranked on that rebalance day.
- Hold the 30 lowest-volatility stocks, equal fixed notional (Rs 1,00,000 each).
- Keep a held stock that stays in the 30; sell one that leaves; buy one that enters.
- All orders at the next session's open. No stop, no target, no maximum holding period.
- Kill criterion: net expectancy per trade zero or negative after costs; the platform's
  hard gates decide the verdict.
