The strategy in the video is a first 5-minute candle intraday setup for Indian stocks. Here’s it in English:

The problem with the common approach:  
Most traders just mark the high and low of the first 5-minute candle and take an entry when price breaks above or below it. Akhil says this leads to repeated stop-loss hits because there’s no real analysis behind it — it’s often just “stop-loss hunting.”

His improved method:
1. Mark the range - Note the high and low of the first 5-minute candle of the day.
2. Wait for consolidation - Wait for the price to consolidate inside that range for at least 1 hour. The price should not break outside the range during this period.
3. Look for two entry setups:

   - Trap entry: Price briefly breaks out of the range to trigger other traders’ stop losses, then reverses back inside. You enter in the direction of the reversal, with your stop loss just beyond the same candle. Target the opposite end of the range.
   - Breakout entry: After consolidation, price breaks out and makes a new swing high or low. You enter on that break, with stop loss below/above the swing point. Target the other end of the range.
4. Trade within the range - The core idea is to trade inside the first 5-minute candle’s range, not outside it.

Here’s Abhishek Singh’s 9 EMA + Fibonacci Pivot Points intraday strategy translated to English:
Setup
- Chart: 5-minute timeframe
- Indicators: 
    - 9-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
    - Pivot Points Standard, set to Fibonacci type
- Platform example: TradingView, using Nifty 50 Index

How to set it up on TradingView:
1. Open TradingView and load the chart for the index or stock you trade, like Bank Nifty.
2. Go to Indicators → search for “Moving Average Exponential” → add it → set the length to 9.
3. Go to Indicators again → search for “Pivot Points Standard” → add it → change the type setting from Traditional to Fibonacci.

Entry Rules
- Long/Buy Entry:  
  When price comes down to a support zone and reverses, wait for a bullish candle to break above both the 9 EMA line and the Pivot line at the same time. Enter on that breakout.
- Short/Sell Entry:  
  When price comes up to a resistance zone and reverses, wait for a bearish candle to break below both the 9 EMA line and the Pivot line at the same time. Enter on that breakout.

Target & Risk
- Target: 1:2 to 1:3 Risk-Reward ratio. So if your stop loss is ₹10, aim for ₹20-₹30 profit.
- Stop Loss: Not explicitly stated in the video, but typically placed below the breakout candle for longs, and above it for shorts.

Key notes from the creator:
- He claims the accuracy is around 60-70% and says it’s simple enough that even a 10-year-old could use it.
- Advises backtesting the strategy for 2-3 months with discipline before trading live.
- This is for educational purposes only and carries trading risk.

He uses Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty as examples, but says you can apply it to any index or stock you trade.

Here’s the “Secret Bullish Breakout Strategy” translated into English:

*The 6 Confirmations Before Entry:*
A genuine bullish breakout needs more than just price breaking out. Check these 6 conditions:

1. *Previous Day High (PDH) breakout* – Price should break above yesterday’s high.
2. *First 5-minute candle high breakout* – Price should break above the high of today’s first 5-minute candle.  
3. *EMA Alignment* – The 10 EMA should be above the 20 EMA, and the 20 EMA should be above the 50 EMA. This shows a bullish trend alignment.
4. *ADX above 25* – Average Directional Index should be above 25, which confirms there’s strong momentum in the trend.
5. *Price above VWAP* – Price should be trading above the Volume Weighted Average Price, indicating buyers are in control.
6. *Volume spike* – The breakout candle’s volume should be at least 1.5x the average volume.

*How it’s used:*
Once all 6 conditions are met, it signals a high-quality momentum breakout. In the video example on Adani Total Gas Ltd (ATGL), the indicator gave an alert at 9:55 AM when all conditions were confirmed, and momentum followed right after.

*Note from the creator:*

The video is already in English, but here’s the strategy explanation in plain text:

*Core idea:* A 9/21 EMA bull cross alone isn’t a tradeable setup. What matters is the price structure around the cross.

*The 3 examples shown:*

1. *First cross – Skip it*  
   Price is still trapped under a resistance level. Even though the 9 EMA crossed above the 21 EMA, buyers don’t have control yet, so the breakout is likely to fail.

2. *Second cross – Too late*  
   The big move has already happened — the stock is up +37% before the cross occurs. Entering here means you’re buying at an extended level with little upside left.

3. *Third cross – The valid setup
   This is the one worth trading. The sequence is:
   - *Cross:* 9 EMA crosses above 21 EMA
   - *Base:* Price forms a tight consolidation/base after the cross
   - *Pullback:* Price pulls back toward the EMA zone  
   - *Defense:* Buyers step in and hold/defend the EMA zone
   - *Breakout:* Once price breaks out of the base, that’s the actual entry signal

*Key takeaway:* The EMA cross is just the trigger. The real setup is _cross → base → pullback → buyers defending the EMA zone → breakout_.

Here’s Omar Agag’s $500 day-trading strategy broken down step by step:

*Timeframes:* 4-hour chart + 15-minute chart  
*Goal:* Make $500 on a $100k prop firm account using a 2:1 risk-reward ratio

*Step 1: Find the setup on the 4-hour chart*
- Look for a *4-hour engulfing candle* that also *sweeps the low of the previous candle*.  
  _What this means:_ The price drops below the prior candle’s low, triggers stop losses, then reverses strongly upward. This is called a “liquidity sweep + rejection.”
- Once you see it, mark a *buy zone* — specifically the _lower half_ of that engulfing candle’s body. That’s your area of interest.

*Step 2: Drop to the 15-minute chart*
- Wait for price to *pull back into the buy zone* you marked on the 4H.
- Watch for *market structure to realign bullish* — basically a small break or higher low showing buyers are back in control. You don’t need a complex signal, just a simple break structure.

*Step 3: Entry & Risk Management*
- *Entry:* Enter when price pulls back into the buy zone and market structure confirms bullish continuation.
- *Stop Loss:* Place it below the buy zone / below the sweep low.
- *Take Profit:* Aim for a *2:1 risk-reward*. 
  _Example math:_ On a $100k account, risk 0.25% = $250. A 2R win = $500 profit.

*Key point from Omar:*  
The math is the easy part. The hard part is executing consistently and not over-risking. You don’t need a huge win once a month — small 2R wins are enough.
