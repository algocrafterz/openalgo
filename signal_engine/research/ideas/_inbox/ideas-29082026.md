
Strategy: VWAP Strategy:
Here’s Garvit’s VWAP strategy translated into a clear, step-by-step implementation format:

### *What VWAP is*
*VWAP = Volume Weighted Average Price* = The session’s “fair price”.
Big institutional algos are programmed to trade around this line all day.
- *Price above VWAP = Buyers are in control*
- *Price below VWAP = Sellers are in control*

---

### *Rule 1: Qualified Break → First Pullback Entry* _(Bullish setup)_

This is for going long when price breaks above VWAP.

*Step 1: Check if the break is “qualified”* — not every break counts. It must pass all 3 checks:
1. *Strong close above VWAP* → The candle must _close_ above VWAP, not just wick above it.
2. *Volume spike on the break* → Higher volume than recent candles when it breaks out.
3. *Structure break* → Price breaks and takes out the previous swing high.

*Step 2: Entry*
Once the break is qualified, wait for the *first pullback to VWAP* and enter long on that touch.
_Logic:_ Algos that missed the initial move will buy at VWAP since it’s now seen as support.

*Step 3: Stop Loss & Target*
- Stop loss: Below VWAP or below the pullback low
- Target: Previous swing high or use a 2:1 risk-reward

---

### *Rule 2: Regime Change* _(Bearish shift)_

This is for recognizing when control flips from buyers to sellers.

*Step 1: Look for acceptance below VWAP on the 5-min chart*
- *3 consecutive 5-min candles close below VWAP* +
- *A retest that holds below VWAP* = Control has shifted to sellers.

*Step 2: Action*
Adjust your bias/position. A single close below VWAP isn’t enough — you need confirmation.

---

### *Key Mindset from Garvit*
Institutions “hug” the VWAP line with billions of dollars. Retail traders often chase candles instead. The goal is to trade _with_ institutional flow, not against it.

---

### *How to practice before risking money*
1. Open a 5-minute chart
2. Add the VWAP indicator
3. Study historical price action to see how price reacts to VWAP in past sessions

Strategy: Double Top / Double Bottom Strategy:

The speaker’s “number one trade” is built around *double top and double bottom chart patterns*, paired with key price levels and two moving averages. He’s dismissing other indicators like RSI, MACD, Fibonacci, and ICT concepts as unnecessary. Here’s the breakdown of how he describes it:

### 1. *The Core Pattern: Double Top / Double Bottom*
- *Double Top:* Price makes a high, pulls back, then rallies back up to that same high level but fails to break through and reverses downward. It looks like an “M”.
- *Double Bottom:* Price makes a low, bounces up, then drops back to that same low level but fails to break lower and reverses upward. It looks like a “W”.
- He calls this the most consistent, high-probability setup he’s used over 34 years of training professional traders.

### 2. *Where to look for these patterns - The Hierarchy of Levels*
He says you don’t just draw these anywhere. You check them in this order of importance:
1. *Previous Day High / Low* – The most important. A double top/bottom forming at yesterday’s high or low is the primary setup.
2. *Pre-market High / Low* – If the previous day level doesn’t play out, the next place to watch is the high or low made before the regular market open.
3. *Intraday High / Low* – The third priority is the highest or lowest point made during the same trading session.

### 3. *The Confirmation Trigger*
The pattern alone isn’t the entry. He waits for a *reversal off two key levels*:
- *VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)* – The average price based on volume for the day. A reversal off VWAP signals institutional buying/selling pressure.
- *200-day Moving Average* – A major long-term trend level. A bounce or rejection off the 200 MA adds confluence to the trade.

So the full idea is: _Price forms a double top/bottom at a significant high/low level, then you look for it to reverse right at VWAP or the 200 MA._

### 4. *His Philosophy*
He argues you don’t need a 500-page manual or trading bots. Just this one pattern + those two levels is enough if you execute it consistently. He’s positioning it as a simple, repeatable setup he’s used to train traders for decades.
